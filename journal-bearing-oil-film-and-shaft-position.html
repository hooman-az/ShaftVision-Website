Journal Bearing Oil Film and Average Shaft Position

A journal bearing supports a rotating shaft on a pressurised lubricant film. Understanding the relationship between bearing clearance, journal eccentricity and average shaft position is essential when interpreting proximity-probe DC measurements.

Important distinction

Average shaft position is not the same quantity as oil-film thickness. A DC shaft-position assessment locates the journal relative to a defined measurement reference. Determining minimum film thickness requires the bearing geometry and an appropriate bearing/operating model; it must not be inferred from a DC position number alone.

1. Journal centre, bearing centre and radial clearance

The bearing bore defines a geometric centre, while the journal centre may be offset from it during operation. The offset between these centres is commonly described as journal eccentricity. Radial clearance describes the available radial space between the journal and bearing surfaces under the stated dimensional convention.

These quantities must be kept distinct. A measured shaft position is meaningful only when its coordinate origin, probe orientation and reference condition are known. The bearing geometric centre should not silently replace the installed probe's mechanical rest/reference position.

2. Why an oil film forms

In a hydrodynamic journal bearing, relative motion between the journal and bearing, together with lubricant viscosity and the converging film geometry, generates pressure in the lubricant. The resulting pressure distribution supports the applied load. The journal therefore operates eccentrically in many normal conditions rather than remaining exactly concentric with the bearing bore.

Load, speed, lubricant properties, temperature, supply conditions, bearing dimensions and surface condition all influence bearing behaviour. A change in operating position may be relevant to an investigation, but it does not by itself identify the cause of the change.

3. What proximity-probe DC readings tell you

An eddy-current proximity probe measures the gap between its tip and the shaft target within its calibrated range. The DC component represents the slowly varying or average gap; the AC component carries dynamic motion. With two correctly installed X-Y probes, validated sensitivity and polarity, known probe angles and a confirmed reference condition, DC readings can be used to calculate relative shaft-position change in the machine coordinate system.

Reference-based assessment

For each channel, the voltage difference between the running condition and its confirmed reference is converted to displacement using the applicable probe sensitivity and calibration polarity. The channel contributions are then resolved using the installed probe angles. The reference readings remain the baseline for the calculation.

4. What DC position cannot establish on its own

  • It does not directly measure minimum oil-film thickness.
  • It does not quantify dynamic vibration amplitude or orbit shape.
  • It does not independently prove bearing wear, rub, oil whirl or misalignment.
  • It does not explain why a position shift occurred without operating context and corroborating evidence.

For these questions, engineers may need bearing dimensions, load and speed data, lubricant information, temperature, dynamic vibration measurements, inspection findings and the machine's operating history.

5. Interpreting position relative to clearance

Once the coordinate reference and bearing dimensions are established, the calculated journal position can be compared with the applicable clearance boundary. A circular model and an elliptical model are not interchangeable: the chosen geometry must represent the bearing data and assumptions used for the assessment. The direction and magnitude of a position change should be reported with the reference convention clearly stated.

A point approaching or crossing an entered clearance boundary is an engineering prompt for review, not a standalone root-cause diagnosis. Verify probe calibration, polarity, mounting, reference readings and bearing data before drawing a conclusion.

Frequently asked questions

Does average shaft centerline directly measure oil-film thickness?

No. Average shaft position describes rotor location relative to a defined reference. Oil-film thickness depends on the local bearing geometry and the minimum film region; it cannot be established from a DC position value alone without a suitable bearing model and additional information.

What is shaft eccentricity in a journal bearing?

Shaft eccentricity is the offset between the journal centre and the bearing geometric centre. Its magnitude and direction are interpreted relative to the bearing clearance and the coordinate convention.

Can proximity-probe DC readings be used to calculate shaft position?

Yes, when probe sensitivity, polarity, installed angles, reference readings and the coordinate system are known and checked. The readings establish position change relative to the confirmed reference condition.

Does a shaft centerline plot replace vibration analysis?

No. Average shaft centerline uses DC position information. Dynamic vibration and orbit analysis use AC signal information and answer different diagnostic questions.
