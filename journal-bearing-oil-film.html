Fluid-film bearings · Rotor position

Journal Bearing, Oil Film and Shaft Position

A practical engineering guide to how a hydrodynamic journal bearing supports a rotor, why the journal operates eccentrically and how shaft-position measurements should be interpreted alongside bearing geometry.

Engineering distinction

The shaft centreline is a description of the rotor's average position. It is not, by itself, a direct measurement of minimum oil-film thickness, bearing health or dynamic stability. Those conclusions require additional geometry, operating data and diagnostic evidence.

How a hydrodynamic journal bearing supports a rotor

In a fluid-film journal bearing, relative motion between the rotating journal and bearing surface draws lubricant into a converging clearance. The resulting pressure distribution supports the applied load and establishes an operating equilibrium. The journal therefore commonly runs eccentrically rather than exactly at the bearing's geometric centre.

What influences the operating shaft position?

  • Applied load and direction: radial load and its orientation influence the equilibrium position.
  • Rotational speed: speed affects the hydrodynamic film and the bearing's operating response.
  • Lubricant properties: viscosity, temperature and supply conditions influence film formation.
  • Bearing geometry: clearance, length-to-diameter ratio, groove arrangement and surface condition matter.
  • Machine state: thermal growth, alignment, transient conditions and rotor-system forces can change measured position.

Radial clearance, eccentricity and minimum geometric gap

For an ideal circular journal and bearing, radial clearance is the difference between the bearing radius and journal radius. If only diametral clearance is supplied, radial clearance is one half of that value. Keeping these definitions separate is essential: a diametral-clearance value must not be entered or interpreted as a radial clearance.

Let c be radial clearance and e be the distance between the journal centre and bearing centre. The eccentricity ratio is ε = e / c. For the idealized circular geometry, the minimum geometric surface gap is hmin = c − e, provided the journal remains within the bearing boundary. This is a geometric relationship, not a complete hydrodynamic calculation; it does not account for elastic deformation, non-circular geometry, misalignment, grooves or local surface damage.

Do not confuse clearance with oil-film thickness

Radial clearance describes the bearing's geometric space. Minimum film thickness describes the local operating lubricant gap between the journal and bearing surfaces. Although the idealized geometry relates the two through eccentricity, a DC shaft-position vector alone is not enough to certify actual film thickness or bearing condition.

Why position is not the same as oil-film thickness

A proximity-probe system measures the local gap between its probe tip and the shaft target. Converting two orthogonal probe readings into a shaft-position vector provides a view of mean rotor location in the chosen coordinate system. Minimum film thickness is a different geometric quantity: it depends on the journal and bearing surfaces, eccentricity, bearing dimensions and the direction of the minimum clearance. It should not be inferred from one DC voltage or a shaft-centreline angle alone.

Using bearing-clearance geometry responsibly

A circular clearance representation assumes a particular simplified geometry. An elliptical or otherwise non-circular representation may be more appropriate when supported by the bearing design information. The selected model, reference origin and clearance values should be documented. A calculated point outside an entered boundary is a geometric finding relative to those inputs; it is not a substitute for inspection or a complete bearing diagnosis.

Practical interpretation checklist

  1. Confirm the bearing type, dimensions and actual clearance information.
  2. Verify the mechanical reference condition and probe mounting geometry.
  3. Check probe sensitivity, polarity and channel orientation.
  4. Record machine speed, load, oil temperature and relevant operating state.
  5. Interpret the position together with vibration, process trends, temperatures and inspection findings.

Frequently asked questions

Does average shaft centerline directly measure oil-film thickness?

No. Average shaft position describes rotor location relative to a defined reference. Oil-film thickness depends on local bearing geometry and the minimum film region; it cannot be established from a DC position value alone without a suitable bearing model and additional information.

What is shaft eccentricity in a journal bearing?

Shaft eccentricity is the offset between the journal centre and the bearing geometric centre. Its magnitude and direction are interpreted relative to bearing clearance and the coordinate convention.

Can proximity-probe DC readings calculate shaft position?

Yes, when probe sensitivity, polarity, installed angles, reference readings and coordinate system are known and checked. The readings establish position change relative to the confirmed reference condition.

Does a shaft centerline plot replace vibration analysis?

No. Average shaft centerline uses DC position information. Dynamic vibration and orbit analysis use AC signal information and answer different diagnostic questions.
