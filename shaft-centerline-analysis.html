Shaft Centerline Analysis

Average Shaft Centerline analysis describes where a rotor's mean radial position sits within its bearing clearance. ShaftVision is a focused engineering tool for assessing this position from X and Y proximity-probe DC readings, a confirmed reference/rest condition and bearing-clearance information.

Important measurement distinction

The average shaft centerline is derived from the DC component of proximity-probe signals. AC dynamic vibration and orbit analysis use different signal information. ShaftVision Core Fault Diagnostics evaluates DC Position; it is not an orbit analyzer and does not process AC waveform or full-spectrum files.

What does a shaft centerline plot tell an engineer?

In a fluid-film journal bearing, the rotor does not normally operate exactly at the geometric centre of the bearing. Its average position is influenced by the machine's operating condition, bearing geometry, load and rotor-system characteristics. A shaft centerline view helps an engineer understand the radial location of that average position relative to a defined reference and the available bearing clearance.

What data is needed?

  • X and Y proximity-probe DC gap readings for the relevant measurement condition.
  • A confirmed reference or rest position, with the reference readings recorded consistently.
  • Probe orientation/angles and the applicable probe sensitivity.
  • Bearing-clearance information and the geometry needed to interpret radial position.

How ShaftVision approaches DC position

ShaftVision provides a structured workflow for recording the reference condition, entering operating DC readings, calculating average shaft-centerline position from the probe geometry, and reviewing the result in relation to bearing clearance. The purpose is to make the position assessment clear and traceable—not to substitute dynamic vibration analysis or a complete machinery diagnostic investigation.
