Eddy-Current Proximity Probe Technology

Understand how proximity-probe systems measure shaft gap, what their DC output represents and why calibration, polarity and installed X-Y probe angles matter.

Measurement principle

An eddy-current proximity probe system measures the gap to a conductive shaft target. The complete measurement chain—probe, extension cable and proximitor/driver—must be treated as a calibrated system. The voltage-to-distance relationship depends on the specific system and target material.

What the DC output represents

The DC component of the driver output corresponds to the average probe-to-target gap within the calibrated operating range. Changes in DC voltage can be converted to changes in gap using the applicable system sensitivity and polarity. The DC value is not an AC vibration amplitude and should not be interpreted as one.

Why calibration and target material matter

Probe sensitivity is not a universal constant for every installation. Confirm the manufacturer's calibration, probe and extension-cable combination, driver compatibility, target material and any applicable calibration curve. A nominal sensitivity sometimes used in industry must never replace the calibration value for the installed channel.

X-Y probe geometry

Two radial probes are commonly installed approximately orthogonally to observe shaft motion in two directions. Their actual installed angles, channel labels and polarity define how measured gap changes map into machine coordinates. Do not assume that channel X is horizontal, channel Y is vertical, or that a particular voltage increase always means movement toward the bearing centre without checking the installation convention.

Reference-to-running calculation

For each channel, calculate the voltage difference between the operating condition and the confirmed reference condition. Convert that difference to displacement using the channel's calibrated sensitivity and polarity, then resolve the contribution using the probe's installed angle. Combine the two contributions in the agreed coordinate system. This yields a relative position change; it does not justify silently replacing the measured reference with an assumed zero or bearing-centre position.

Illustrative relationship

Displacement change = (operating voltage − reference voltage) ÷ calibrated sensitivity, with the installed polarity convention applied. This relationship is channel-specific and should be used only within the calibrated linear range.

Example: converting voltage change to displacement

Assume, for illustration only, that a specific installed channel has a verified sensitivity of 200 mV/mil. A 100 mV change between the operating and reference readings corresponds to 0.5 mil of gap change in magnitude: 100 ÷ 200 = 0.5 mil. Whether that represents an increase or decrease in gap depends on the calibrated channel polarity.

This example is not a universal probe specification. Actual sensitivity must be taken from the calibration applicable to the installed probe, extension cable, driver and shaft target. Do not transfer a value from another machine or channel without verification.

Common measurement errors

  • Using a nominal sensitivity instead of the calibration value for the installed system.
  • Reversing polarity or entering a probe angle from the wrong viewing direction.
  • Mixing channel labels or confusing the probe's physical orientation with the display coordinate convention.
  • Comparing readings taken with different reference conditions or machine states.
  • Assuming DC position data can replace AC vibration, orbit or transient waveform analysis.

Field verification checklist

  1. Identify the probe, extension cable and driver model for each channel.
  2. Verify calibration and target material compatibility.
  3. Confirm channel polarity and installed angular orientation.
  4. Record the rest/reference and operating voltages under documented conditions.
  5. Check the calculated direction against known machine geometry before drawing conclusions.

ShaftVision is designed to support Average Shaft Centerline (DC Position) calculations from X-Y probe readings and a confirmed reference. The quality of the result depends on accurate installation data and calibration inputs supplied by the engineer.

Frequently asked questions

Is 200 mV/mil the correct sensitivity for every proximity probe?

No. Sensitivity depends on the installed probe, extension cable, driver and calibration. Use the verified channel-specific calibration value rather than assuming a nominal sensitivity.

How is a DC voltage change converted to displacement?

Divide the operating-minus-reference voltage difference by the calibrated sensitivity, applying the channel polarity convention. The result is valid only within the calibrated linear range.

Why do probe angle and polarity matter?

Probe angle defines the direction of the measured gap change in machine coordinates, while polarity defines its sign. Incorrect settings can reverse or rotate the calculated shaft-position vector.

Can proximity-probe DC readings replace vibration data?

No. DC readings describe average probe-to-shaft gap. AC vibration data describes dynamic shaft motion and requires a separate analysis.
